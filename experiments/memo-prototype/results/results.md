# Memo-prototype benchmark: lib_memo (M) vs hand-rolled space cache (S)

All runs on the PR-165 PeTTa runtime (`/home/yab/PeTTa-pr165/run.sh`, commit
`b1f6f49`), sequential (never parallel), 5 reps per cell, invoked by
`run-bench.sh` from drivers generated by `gen-driver.py` out of
`driver-template.metta`. Raw logs: `results/raw/<workload>_<variant>[_<strategy>_<limit>]_rep<i>.log`
(+ `.time.txt` per-rep process wall-clock cross-check).

**Timing method.** Primary measurement is in-script: the driver wraps ONLY the
candidate fold (replay-stream iteration + evalCol/evalColCached + checksum
accumulation) between two `(current-time)` calls (`BENCH_T0`/`BENCH_T1`
markers), excluding process startup, imports, replay-stream loading, and table
installation. No helper `.pl` was needed: `current-time` (SWI `get_time/1`) is
a native PR-165 builtin, verified with a 2-line probe before use. Each rep was
additionally wrapped in bash's `time` builtin as a process-level cross-check;
GNU `/usr/bin/time` is **not installed** on this machine, so no max-RSS was
collected (see threats to validity).

**Workload / cache-key facts** (from capture phase + observed entry counts):

| workload | rows | candidates | distinct candidates | distinct subtree cache keys (observed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parity3 | 8 | 7031 | 977 | 2420 |
| majority3 | 8 | 914 | 192 | 398 |
| mux6 | 64 | 3798 | 1125 | 2618 |
| rand12 | 4096 | 1843 | 652 | 1384 |

Note the last column: both kernels cache **subtrees**, not whole candidates,
so the number of distinct cache keys is ~2-2.5x the distinct-candidate count.
The plan's "unique-limit = distinct-count" arm used the distinct **candidate**
counts (192/1125/977/652), which therefore lands **below** the true key count —
those cells run under mild-to-moderate eviction pressure rather than unbounded.
This is called out per-cell below and in the eviction section.

## 1. Is memoize faster or slower than the hand-rolled space cache?

**It depends on the size of the cached values (result columns), and the
crossover is clearly visible in this data.**

- **Tiny columns (8 rows): S wins.** majority3: S 0.0441 s vs best-M 0.0543 s
  (lru, ample capacity) — S is ~19% faster. parity3: S 0.68 s (min) vs
  M 0.93 s (min) — S ~27% faster, though the M cell was handicapped by
  eviction pressure (misses 3716 vs 2420), so treat the parity3 delta as an
  upper bound on lib_memo's disadvantage there.
- **Medium columns (64 rows): roughly a wash, slight M edge unbounded.**
  mux6 with identical stats (6838/2618 at limit 10000): M min 1.00 s vs
  S min 1.22 s → M ~18% faster on the noise-robust statistic; medians overlap
  within noise.
- **Large columns (4096 rows): M wins decisively.** rand12: M 25.7 s vs
  S 39.9 s → **M is ~1.55x faster** — *despite* M running with eviction
  pressure (limit 652 < 1384 keys, costing it 234 extra recomputations of
  4096-cell columns) while S was unbounded.

Mechanism (consistent with the runtimes' source): variant S's lookup is
`collapse (match &protoSubtreeCache (subtree $exp $v) $v)` — a findall over
the space with structural unification, which copies the full stored column on
every hit and scans candidate atoms; the per-lookup cost grows with both entry
count and column size. lib_memo's `metta_memo_entry/5` lookup is a
first-argument-indexed Prolog fact probe with a quantized key — near-constant
per lookup — plus fixed bookkeeping overhead (queue + stats, and for wtinylfu
a Count-Min Sketch update). On 8-row tables the bookkeeping dominates and S's
lean idiom wins; once columns are 4096 cells, S's copy/scan cost dwarfs M's
bookkeeping.

## 2. Hit-rate comparison

**Identical when unbounded — verified exactly.** Wherever M had capacity for
all keys, its counters matched S bit-for-bit:

- majority3, M lru/wtinylfu @ 1000 and 10000: 1264 hits / 398 misses — exactly
  S's numbers (hit-rate 0.761).
- mux6, M lru/wtinylfu @ 10000: 6838 / 2618 — exactly S's numbers (0.723).

This confirms key-shape parity: both kernels key on the identical ground
wrapped subtree expression and take the identical Grounded-leaf bypass, so
the sequence of cacheable calls is the same. Every divergence in the tables
is fully explained by capacity: any cell with limit < distinct-key-count shows
misses > distinct-keys (evicted-then-recomputed) and correspondingly extra
hits from re-stored entries. No unexplained divergence was observed.

## 3. Eviction sensitivity

- **Thrashing at unique-limit 100** (well below key counts of 398/2618):
  - majority3 lru: misses 961 = 2.4x the 398 distinct keys; wall 0.197 s =
    3.6x slower than lru-unbounded. wtinylfu: misses 755 (1.9x), wall 0.0775 s
    — the sketch-based admission policy contains the thrash markedly better
    (21% fewer misses, 2.5x faster wall than lru at the same starved limit).
  - mux6 lru: misses 8102 = 3.1x of 2618; wtinylfu: 6886 (2.6x), and wall
    min 1.25 s vs lru's 1.44 s. Same pattern: **under starved capacity,
    wtinylfu beats lru** on both misses and wall time.
- **unique-limit = distinct-candidate-count** (192 / 1125 — i.e. ~half the true
  subtree key count): mild pressure only. majority3: misses 552 (1.39x);
  mux6 lru: 2792 (1.07x), mux6 wtinylfu: 3220 (1.23x). Wall degrades a few
  percent to ~25% vs unbounded. Notably lru handles this near-capacity regime
  slightly better than wtinylfu on misses for mux6 (2792 vs 3220): with the
  hot set barely not fitting, strict recency evicts less pessimally than
  frequency-based admission, which keeps rejecting newly-stored keys whose
  frequency estimate hasn't accumulated yet.
- **wtinylfu vs lru at ample capacity: lru is faster despite identical
  hit-rates.** majority3 @ 10000: lru 0.0543 s vs wtinylfu 0.1216 s — a
  **2.2x wall-time penalty with the exact same 1264/398 stats**, i.e. pure
  Count-Min-Sketch/admission bookkeeping overhead, very visible on a workload
  of ~1662 cache calls totalling ~54 ms. On mux6 the same effect exists but
  drowns in noise (min 0.999 vs 1.009 s).
- **Anomaly worth flagging**: majority3 wtinylfu @ 100 (0.0775 s) is *faster*
  than wtinylfu @ 1000/10000 (~0.12 s) even though it does 357 extra column
  recomputations. Consistent explanation: at limit 100 the admission policy
  rejects most store attempts outright (cheap), while at ample capacity every
  miss pays the full store + queue + sketch maintenance path; with 8-row
  columns recomputation is cheaper than lib_memo's store path. Also observed:
  one rep each of `majority3_memo_lru_100` (0.087 s vs 0.20 s siblings) and
  `majority3_memo_wtinylfu_1000` (0.057 s vs 0.12 s siblings) ran in a
  distinctly faster mode — bimodality on sub-0.2 s cells; medians quoted are
  the slow (majority) mode.

## 4. Threats to validity

- **Translator echo overhead**: the PR-165 runner has no silent flag; every
  top-level form (including thousands of replay `add-atom` lines) is echoed
  with its Prolog translation. All stdout was redirected to the raw logs. The
  in-script T0/T1 window excludes load/echo time entirely, and the echo cost
  is identical for S and M on the same replay file, so S-vs-M deltas are
  unaffected; only the process-level `.time.txt` numbers carry this constant.
- **Machine noise / single machine**: single desktop, no isolation. mux6 and
  parity3 cells show bimodal reps (~2x slow mode; stdev up to 0.7 s on ~1.2 s
  medians). Per-rep minima are reported alongside medians for those tables.
  rand12 and majority3 (except noted anomalies) were stable (stdev/median
  ≤ 6%). No max-RSS data: GNU `/usr/bin/time` is not installed; memory
  comparison remains open.
- **Runtime pin**: all numbers are for PeTTa-pr165 at commit `b1f6f49`
  (lib_memo is PR-165-only). Variant S also runs on PeTTaV1, but no PeTTaV1
  timings are part of this matrix.
- **Key-shape parity decision**: both kernels key on the raw ground wrapped
  expression (no quote, AND/OR/NOT undefined ⇒ inert), table identity is NOT
  part of the key (fixed-table-per-process discipline, staleness demonstrated
  in the correctness harnesses). The unbounded stats equality above is the
  empirical proof of parity.
- **unique-limit arm mismatch vs true key count**: the "distinct-count" limits
  were distinct *candidates*, but keys are *subtrees* (~2-2.5x more), so those
  cells (192/1125/977/652) measured mild eviction pressure, not exact-fit
  capacity. In particular the parity3 and rand12 M cells are handicapped
  relative to S; rand12's M win is therefore conservative.
- **In-script vs process timing**: in-script marker deltas are the primary
  numbers; process `real` times (bash `time`) corroborate every ranking
  reported (e.g. rand12: 26.7 s vs 41.1 s process medians for M vs S).
- **Checksum strength**: the checksum (total True cells across all result
  columns) would not catch a permutation of cells within a column that
  preserves the True-count; the phase-3 correctness harnesses (exact expected
  columns, hand-computed) cover exact-value correctness.

## Checksum verification

Every one of the 22 cells x 5 reps produced the workload's expected checksum —
majority3 3977, mux6 140043, parity3 30354, rand12 3912064 — with **zero
mismatches** across variants, strategies, limits, and reps.
